# ShelfLife Inventory Application

## About the Project

ShelfLife is a restaurant inventory application designed to make every count count. The idea came from situations I experienced while managing restaurants, including an emergency trip to Walmart to buy practically every egg in the store and another time when I had to replace produce even though the previous night’s count said we were fine. ShelfLife gives managers one simple place to see what they have, what is running low, and what needs attention before it becomes tomorrow’s emergency.

## Design and User Needs

The app includes account creation and login, an inventory screen, options to add, edit, and delete products, low-stock highlighting, and SMS-alert permission. I kept the dark-purple design clean and made the inventory screen easy to scan because restaurant managers usually do not have time to dig through menus. The most important details—product, quantity, unit, location, and low-stock status—are visible together.

## Development and Testing

I approached the project one piece at a time, beginning with the visual layouts and then connecting the working features. I tested account creation, login, incorrect credentials, adding and editing products, deleting an item, low-stock alerts, data remaining after restarting the app, and both responses to the SMS-permission request. Testing was important because it revealed problems that were not always part of the app itself, including emulator graphics trouble and OneDrive temporarily locking build files. Working through those challenges taught me to slow down, isolate the problem, and test again instead of changing everything at once.

## Success and Future Direction

I am most proud of the inventory screen because it provides a useful manager’s view without becoming complicated. The low-stock highlighting makes potential problems easy to spot, and the simple plus button keeps adding inventory straightforward. In the future, I would add selectable storage areas and allow an employee to move seasonal products between locations more easily. ShelfLife could also grow into an affordable subscription for independent restaurants and eventually offer enterprise features such as shared locations, ordering support, point-of-sale integration, and AI-assisted identification of unusual inventory counts.
